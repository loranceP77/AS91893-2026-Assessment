Larb is one of my favourites, mainly because of its unique flavor and because it is the signature dish from the culture I am from. It is a type of meat salad, it is typically prepared with minced meat, fish sauce, lime juice, toasted rice, and fresh herbs. It is traditionally served with sticky rice and fresh vegetables.

The combination of savory meat, tangy lime juice, and fragrant herbs creates a profile that is both refreshing and deeply satisfying.

Originating in Laos and Thailand centuries ago, the name "Larb" is a homonym for fortune and luck in the Lao and Thai languages. This makes it a celebratory staple often served at weddings, housewarmings, and festivals to bring prosperity to guests.

It is a incredibly versatile dish; while chicken, pork, or beef are traditional, it can easily be adapted with tofu for vegetarians. Overall, larb is a delicious and healthy meal that I highly recommend trying.

Fun Facts below!

The signature nutty flavor comes from Khao Khua—raw
glutinous rice that is toasted until golden and ground
into a coarse powder.

While the famous version is sour and herbal (Isan style),
Northern Thai (Lanna) larb is savory and bitter, often
using dried spices and even fresh blood.

While pork and chicken are common, larb can be made with almost
any protein, including duck, water buffalo, fish, or mushrooms.
In Laos, "Larb Pa" (fish larb) is particularly prized in
riverside communities.
