Pizza

Pizza is a classic that most people love, and for good reason. Its combination of a crispy crust, savory sauce, melted cheese, and an endless variety of toppings creates a delicious experience that appeals to almost everyone.

Pizza's origins trace back to Naples, Italy, in the 18th and 19th centuries. It was originally a fast, affordable meal for the working class. In 1889, Queen Margherita visited Naples and enjoyed a pizza created in her honor. Topped with tomatoes, mozzarella, and basil to represent the colors of the Italian flag, it became known as the Margherita pizza. As Italian immigrants moved to the United States, they brought these traditional recipes with them, eventually sharing the dish with the world.

The world's largest pizza, measuring 13,957.77 square feet was created by
Pizza Hut and YouTube creator Airrack in Los Angeles on January 19, 2023.
Named "The Big New Yorker," it used 13,653 lbs of dough, 4,948 lbs
of sauce, and over 8,800 lbs of cheese, with all 68,000 slices donated to charity.

Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba in Naples, Italy, is widely regarded
as the world’s first pizzeria. It opened in 1738 to supply
street vendors but eventually expanded into a sit-down restaurant
in 1830—and it’s still serving pizza today.

The "Louis XIII" is the most expensive pizza in the world.
Created by chef Renato Viola, it costs roughly $12,000. The
dough is aged for 72 hours and the toppings include three types
of caviar, organic buffalo mozzarella, and lobster from Norway.
