Sushi is one of my favorite go-to foods. If I had to choose one food to eat for the rest of my life, it would be sushi. With its wide variety and simple preparation, it’s hard not to love.

But what makes sushi so special? It actually began in Southeast Asia as narezushi, a method of preserving fish by wrapping it in fermented rice. Originally, the rice was discarded and only the sour, preserved fish was consumed. It wasn’t until the 1820s that Japan created nigiri, using fresh fish and seasoned vinegar rice, prioritizing freshness over long fermentation.

The reason I love sushi is the incredible variety, the savory fillings, and the slight tanginess of the rice vinegar. It has a rich history and a wonderful flavor profile. It is also quite accessible; you only need seaweed (nori), rice, rice vinegar, a protein (mainly fish), and a vegetable or fruit like cucumber or avocado.

Overall, sushi is a healthy, delicious, and affordable meal that I think everyone should try.

Fun Facts below!

The word "sushi" actually refers to the vinegared rice (shari), not the fish.
You can have sushi without fish (like Inari or cucumber rolls),
but you can’t have sushi without the specific tangy, seasoned rice.

While chopsticks are the standard for sashimi (plain slices of fish), it is perfectly
acceptable and often preferred—to eat nigiri and maki (rolls) with your hands.
This allows you to feel the texture and ensures the delicate rice structure doesn't fall
apart before it reaches your mouth.

Sushi was the original "Fast Food": During the Edo period in Japan (1800s),
sushi was sold from street stalls as a quick snack for people on the
go—much like a hot dog or a slice of pizza today. It was designed
to be eaten quickly with your hands while standing up.
